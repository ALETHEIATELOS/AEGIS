AEGIS

Institutional Intelligence Infrastructure

A research and engineering stack for building evidence-bound, multi-perspective AI decision systems. AEGIS separates AI execution from institutional intelligence — preserving evidence, independent reasoning, disagreement, uncertainty, decision state, human authority, outcomes, and epistemic memory across consequential decisions.

The principle

AI-generated analysis, recommendation, authorization, and execution are distinct states.

READY FOR HUMAN AUTHORITY ≠ AUTHORIZED ≠ EXECUTED

No model, agent, tool, or runtime may grant itself authority.

What it does

  • Evidence — immutable items with a full provenance chain. Nothing is edited, ever; derivations return new items with lineage.
  • Perspectives — genuinely independent reasoning paths, each bound to the evidence it cited. Independence is enforced by what the system withholds.
  • Conflict — disagreement recorded as a first-class object, unresolved by default. Dissent is carried into the output, not averaged away.
  • Kaleidoscope — reframing through foreign standpoints, structured rebuttal rounds with bounded exposure, deterministic sensitivity analysis, and formal minority reports.
  • Readiness — a deterministic, LLM-free check: READY_FOR_HUMAN_AUTHORITY or NOT_READY with actionable reasons.
  • The gate — a closed state machine. Only a human actor can authorize; only an authorized decision can execute.
  • Memory — append-only records, outcomes, and lessons. The institution learns without rewriting its history.

Try it — the review API

This service runs the acquisition-review domain application. POST an evidence intake, get back a full decision dossier — recommendation, preserved dissent, sensitivity analysis, and minority report. It stops at the gate: the response declares the case ready for human authority. Authorization happens outside the system.

Research questions

AEGIS investigates whether AI systems can: preserve evidence provenance through probabilistic reasoning · maintain genuinely independent reasoning paths · represent unresolved disagreement without forcing consensus · propagate uncertainty through analysis and simulation · reconstruct why a decision changed · keep recommendation, authorization, and execution deterministically distinct · learn from outcomes without corrupting the record · and generalize the architecture across decision domains.
